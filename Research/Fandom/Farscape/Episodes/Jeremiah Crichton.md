# Jeremiah Crichton (Season 1, Episode 14)

"Jeremiah Crichton" is the fourteenth episode of the first season of *Farscape*.[1] Directed by Ian Watson and written by Douglas Heyes, Jr., it first aired in the United States on July 30, 1999, and in the United Kingdom on March 20, 2000.[1]

## Synopsis

Frustrated by life aboard Moya and feeling alienated after the events of preceding episodes, John Crichton takes his module out for a solo flight.[2] While he is away, Moya undergoes unexpected pregnancy complications requiring an emergency Starburst, leaving Crichton stranded without hyperdrive capability.[2] He lands on a nearby lush, tropical planet that features an anomalous energy vortex capable of disabling all advanced mechanical technology.[2] 

Three months later, Crichton has settled into a primitive beach-dwelling existence complete with a mountain-man beard, catching local crab-like creatures known as shaklooms.[2] He interacts with a local human-like tribe known as the Acquarans—specifically Lishala, the chief's daughter, who forms an attachment to him.[2] This creates friction with Rokon, the son of the head Priesten Neera, who intended to marry Lishala and succeed as chief.[2]

Meanwhile, D'Argo and Rygel track down Crichton's module on a nearby world and descend via transport pod, only to be similarly stranded when their equipment and weapons are drained by the planetary energy vortex.[2] D'Argo rescues Crichton from a hostile ambush by Rokon's faction.[2] 

## Canon & Lore Revelations

- **The Acquaran Colony:** The human-like inhabitants of the planet are revealed to be descendants of colonists sent out by Hynerian Dominar Rygel XVI's ancestor, Rygel X.[3]
- **The Imperial Betrayal:** Rygel X had intentionally stranded the colonists on the planet to extend his influence, sealing their fate and concealing their origins beneath a manufactured religion controlled by the Priestens.[3]
- **Technology Suppression:** An ancient energy-dampening device maintains the technological blackout on the surface, preserving the Priestens' absolute rule through manufactured ignorance.[3][5unverified]
- **Translator Microbes:** The survival of translator microbes across generations of an isolated, tech-deprived population remains an unexamined background premise of the colony's interaction with Crichton and D'Argo.[3unverified]

## Production & Reception Notes

Showrunner David Kemper and creator Rockne S. O’Bannon noted in the DVD audio commentary (retrospectively titled "When Bad Things Happen to Good Shows") that the episode was heavily inspired by the creators' fondness for the Robert Redford film *Jeremiah Johnson*, leading to a deliberate, standalone wilderness survival diversion for Ben Browder.[3] Critics and fandom retrospectives frequently cite it as one of the series' more whimsical and polarizing standalone diversions during its darker first season.[2][3]
