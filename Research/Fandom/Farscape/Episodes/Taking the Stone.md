# Taking the Stone

> A bounded Chiana episode dossier. This note records the episode's grief, risk, and autonomy conflict while keeping its later thematic interpretation separate from screen-derived plot evidence.

## Episode identity

“Taking the Stone” is season 2, episode 3 of *Farscape*. TheLogBook lists Justin Monjo as writer, Rowan Woods as director, and Subvision as music credit; it identifies Gigi Edgley as Chiana, with Anthony Hayes (Molnon), Peter Scarf (Das), Michela Noonan (Vyna), and Natasha Beaumont (Janixx) among the guest cast.[2]

The episode begins from a loss that Chiana cannot make legible to the rest of Moya's crew. A life-disc that had kept her aware of her brother Nerri's status stops functioning; Chiana reads this as proof of Nerri's death, takes a shuttle, and leaves Moya.[1][2] The event gives a specific episode-level anchor to the wider [[Chiana]] dossier's account of Nerri as her brother and of grief as a recurring pressure on her choices.

## Canonical account

### Departure and the cemetery world

Crichton, Aeryn, and Rygel follow Chiana to a cemetery world occupied by young people living below the surface. The group faces radiation poisoning and treats an extreme ritual—“taking the stone”—as a meaningful alternative to waiting for a painful death.[2] The ritual is a descent or jump through a chasm whose survival mechanism is an unreliable force-field generator; the possibility of death is therefore part of the rite rather than an accidental consequence.[2][3]

The episode's setup does not present Chiana's decision as a simple wish to die. Her grief, anger at the crew's apparent failure to notice her distress, need for self-direction, and immediate attraction to a community outside Moya all matter to the choice. The source material supports those pressures; a diagnosis of Chiana's mental state beyond the episode's actions and dialogue would be interpretation, not established fact.[1][2]

### Molnon and coercive belonging

Molnon tries to recruit Chiana into the community's ritual framework. The episode places that invitation beside a social system in which escape, survival, and group identity are not cleanly separable: the young residents know the radiation threat and still invest the dive with status and meaning.[2][3]

This is important for Chiana's character rather than merely plot machinery. She has fled a Nebari system that turns conformity into a condition of survival. Here she encounters a different community that presents chosen risk as its answer to an inescapable collective fate. The parallel is an analytical reading, not a stated creator explanation; it should not be used to equate Nebari mind cleansing with the cemetery world's ritual culture.

### Crichton's intervention

Crichton rejects the community's fatalism and looks for a practical solution to the radiation threat and the force-field problem. TheLogBook's synopsis records both objectives: he seeks a way to stop the radiation sickness and to dissuade Chiana from the dive.[2] His actions create a real conflict of care: saving someone can be necessary without giving him authority to redefine what their grief or freedom ought to mean.

The episode therefore tests a recurring Moya-crew problem. Chiana wants support without being managed; the crew must decide whether care for her means respecting a destructive choice, intervening against it, or making conditions in which the choice no longer has the same meaning. The specific resolution and all scene-level causal claims should be checked against an authorized episode or production script in a later audit.[unverified]

## Chiana character value

### Nerri's death as a turning point

“Taking the Stone” is the clearest early episode in which Nerri's loss changes Chiana's present behavior rather than operating only as backstory. The life-disc establishes that she remained connected to him despite separation; its failure turns an already precarious relationship with Moya into an acute abandonment crisis.[1][2]

The episode should not be reduced to a lesson in which Chiana merely learns to accept help. Her insistence on leaving, and her resistance to Crichton's attempt to solve the situation for her, preserve the character's central concern with self-determination. The more useful formulation is that the episode makes care, grief, and autonomy collide rather than resolving them into a single moral answer.

### Risk and performance

Chiana's attraction to the dive is consistent with the risk-taking, physical, and socially agile character established after “Durka Returns,” but this episode gives those qualities a mournful context. Her performance moves between provocation, withdrawal, and a search for immediate belonging; it is not simply reckless comic relief.

That reading is analysis. The screen-derived baseline is narrower: she leaves Moya after the disc indicates Nerri's death, joins a group whose ritual may kill her, and becomes the focus of her crewmates' attempt to reach her and address the planet's danger.[1][2]

## Relationship notes

- **[[Nerri]]:** The episode is key evidence that their sibling relationship remained emotionally active during Chiana's time on Moya. It does not, on its own, establish the full history of Nerri's resistance work or the exact circumstances of his death.[1][2]
- **[[John Crichton]]:** Crichton's response combines urgency, protection, and problem-solving. The episode is a useful counterweight to any account that describes their later bond as only flirtation or banter.[2]
- **[[Aeryn Sun]] and [[Rygel XVI]]:** Both join the search, locating Chiana's crisis within the crew rather than treating it as an isolated private plot.[2]
- **[[Moya]]:** Chiana's shuttle departure is an early test of whether Moya is a home she has chosen or merely the place from which she can still run.[1][2]

## Canon versus analysis

### Screen-derived / reference-supported

- A life-disc indicates to Chiana that Nerri has died; she leaves Moya in a shuttle.[1][2]
- Crichton, Aeryn, and Rygel track her to a cemetery world.[2]
- The local youth community faces radiation poisoning and practices the potentially fatal chasm dive called “taking the stone.”[2][3]
- TheLogBook attributes the episode to Justin Monjo and Rowan Woods and records the listed guest cast.[2]

### Analysis

The episode presents grief as a struggle over control of one's own body, risk, and story. Chiana's interest in the ritual is not evidence that she has abandoned the crew; it is evidence that Moya's ordinary forms of comfort cannot yet meet the terms on which she is grieving. The force-field problem turns that emotional conflict into concrete science-fiction action without making the grief itself a technical puzzle to solve.
