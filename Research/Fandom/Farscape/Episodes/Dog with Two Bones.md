# Dog with Two Bones

> **Scope:** canon and production-reference dossier for season 3, episode 22, with interpretation kept separate from episode evidence.

## Episode record

- **Season / episode:** 3.22.[1][3]
- **Written by:** David Kemper.[1][2][5]
- **Directed by:** Andrew Prowse.[1][2][5]
- **Production number:** 10322.[5]
- **First listed UK transmission:** 31 January 2002; first listed US transmission: 26 April 2002.[2][5]
- **Guest cast listed by the episode references:** Tammy MacIntosh as Jool, Melissa Jaffer as the old woman, and Kent McCord as Jack Crichton.[2][5]

## Canon / episode evidence

### Talyn's burial and the rogue Leviathan

Moya carries Talyn's remains to a sacred Leviathan burial ground. A rogue female Leviathan, driven mad after Peacekeepers killed her children, attacks Moya and refuses to allow Talyn—part Peacekeeper, part Leviathan—to be buried there.[1][2]

The rogue Leviathan has already killed or injured other Leviathans, and Moya ultimately asks the crew to stop her. D'Argo's ship and Aeryn's Prowler draw the attacker away from Talyn's remains; D'Argo's weapon completely disintegrates the rogue, allowing Talyn to be laid to rest.[2][5]

Chiana is especially connected to the burial because she helped midwife Talyn's birth. The episode therefore gives Talyn's death a practical funerary resolution while also positioning it as the end of the crew's shared period aboard Moya.[5]

### The crew's divergence

With Scorpius's ship gone and Talyn buried, the crew can pursue separate goals. D'Argo leaves to pursue Macton, Rygel and others discuss returning home, and Aeryn prepares to leave Moya for an ex-Peacekeeper unit focused on protecting people and preventing terrorism.[2]

The BBC's contemporary episode guide frames the episode as a possible “curtain call” for the crew, noting that the characters now have opportunities to go home and that Moya, Pilot, and Jool may follow a different path from D'Argo, Rygel, Aeryn, and Crichton.[1] This is episode framing and contemporary criticism, not a claim that every character's later destination is permanently settled here.

### Crichton's two incompatible homes

Crichton experiences visions of an Earth wedding with Aeryn and an imagined future in which the Moya crew is brought into his old life. The episode contrasts that fantasy with the danger Peacekeepers would pose to Earth and to the people around him.[1][2][5]

The old woman he rescued from the Command Carrier uses herbs and dreamlike interventions to force Crichton to confront what he has been avoiding. He describes himself as a dog with two bones: wanting both Earth and his friends, but risking the loss of both by trying to hold on to incompatible futures.[2][5]

Crichton ultimately tells Aeryn that he loves her and asks to remain with her or go with her. They decide to trust fate and toss a coin; Aeryn leaves while Crichton remains behind. This is the emotional choice at the center of the episode, distinct from the later revelation that Aeryn is pregnant.[2][5]

### Cliffhanger

Harvey unlocks Crichton's suppressed memory of the old woman's warning: Aeryn is pregnant. Before Crichton can return to Moya, a wormhole opens and pulls Moya, Jool, and the old woman through it, leaving Crichton stranded in the Farscape module, out of fuel and alone in space.[2][5]

## Production and interpretation

The episode was written by David Kemper and directed by Andrew Prowse; the production references agree on those credits.[1][2][5] Reactor's retrospective treats the season finale as a contemplative counterpoint to the preceding Command Carrier climax, emphasizing internal loss and the break-up of the crew.

The “dog with two bones” story is an interpretive device within the episode, not an independent technical rule about wormholes or a formal statement of Crichton's future. The episode itself leaves the sudden wormhole unexplained; Reactor explicitly identifies the lack of build-up and explanation as part of the cliffhanger's effect.[2]
