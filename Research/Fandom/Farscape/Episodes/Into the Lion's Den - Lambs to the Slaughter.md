# Into the Lion's Den — Lambs to the Slaughter

> Season 3, episode 20: Moya's crew enters Scorpius's Command Carrier under a negotiated truce, while Scorpius's wormhole project is threatened by Peacekeeper High Command.[1][2]

## Episode identity

- **Production number:** 320 / 10320.[2][3]
- **Written by:** Richard Manning.[1][2][3]
- **Directed by:** Ian Watson.[1][2][3]
- **Original air dates:** 24 January 2002 in the UK and 12 April 2002 in the US.[2][3][4]
- **Following episode:** [[Into the Lion's Den - Wolf in Sheep's Clothing]].
- **Related arc:** [[Episodes/I-Yensch, You-Yensch]], [[Episodes/Fractures]], [[Scorpius]], [[Moya]], [[Talyn]].

## Canon and episode events

### The truce and the Command Carrier

John Crichton and the Moya crew accept Scorpius's offer of diplomatic rights, immunities, and courtesies aboard the Command Carrier. Crichton agrees to help with wormhole research while Scorpius promises assistance to the crew; the arrangement is presented as a tactical truce rather than reconciliation.[1][2][4]

The I-Yensch bracelets link Crichton and Scorpius through their nervous systems, making each vulnerable through the other.[3][4]

The arrangement makes the Command Carrier both a protected diplomatic venue and a coercive trap: Peacekeeper personnel resent the presence of wanted fugitives, while Scorpius's authority is visibly contested from within.[1][2]

### Internal Peacekeeper conflict

The carrier's crew treats Aeryn Sun and Bialar Crais as traitors despite Scorpius's public pardon. Lieutenant Reljik exploits this hostility and arranges an attack on Ka D'Argo; D'Argo survives, and Scorpius threatens severe consequences for further attacks.[2][4]

Commandant Mele-On Grayza arrives from High Command and challenges Scorpius's authority and wormhole project. Scorpius removes her from the ship; she leaves promising to return with full sanction, while separately ordering Reljik to kill Crichton and thereby endanger Scorpius through the bracelet link.[1][2][4]

### Crichton's decision

Scorpius shows Crichton evidence about his own history and reveals that he has located Earth, described as more than sixty cycles away at top speed.[4] Harvey, the neural copy of Scorpius in Crichton's mind, helps conceal Crichton's lies by normalizing his energy signature, but also confirms the seriousness of the Scarran threat as the episode frames it.[2][4]

Crichton begins the episode intending to sabotage the wormhole project. After seeing Scorpius's evidence and considering the consequences of the Scarrans acquiring wormhole technology, he becomes uncertain whether sabotage is still morally or strategically correct. Aeryn reminds him that the other John sacrificed himself to prevent the technology from reaching the wrong hands; Crichton answers that it is now his shift.[4]

### The rest of the crew

- **Aeryn:** Reunites with former colleague Yal Henta, who still identifies with Peacekeeper loyalty and rejects Aeryn's defection. Aeryn says that she retained loyalty, sacrifice, and honour, but did not want to return to the Peacekeepers.[4]
- **Crais:** Is reunited with Lieutenant Darinta Larell, a former lover assigned to spy on him. Their relationship becomes part of the carrier's surveillance and loyalty struggle; the episode leaves her actual motives deliberately unstable.[2][4]
- **D'Argo:** Receives surgery to remove his collarbone restraint rings and intelligence concerning Macton, the killer of Lo'Laan.[2][4]
- **Rygel:** Receives a report that dissent against Dominar Bishan has spread across primary Hynerian home planets, prompting thoughts of reclaiming the throne.[2][4]
- **Chiana and Jool:** Remain with the mission despite the danger. Chiana has a precognitive flash of Pilot screaming before the Retrieval Squad arrives; Jool openly takes the crew's side and participates in the response to D'Argo's attack.[4]
- **Moya and Talyn:** Moya is immobilized and badly damaged while attempting to starburst, then is brought within the Command Carrier's fleet. Talyn is brought aboard for repair, cognitive-system work, and removal of his weapons.[2][4]

## Production and interpretation

The BBC's contemporary guide emphasizes the unstable bargain between Crichton and Scorpius, the hostility of the Command Carrier crew, and the threat posed by superior Peacekeeper authority.[1] Reactor's retrospective identifies the episode's central dramatic turn as Crichton's realization that his personal vendetta against Scorpius has consequences for the Scarran threat and for the wider political balance.[4]

Those readings are interpretation rather than additional canon: the episode itself establishes the truce, the internal Peacekeeper conflict, the bracelet vulnerability, the Earth discovery, and Crichton's hesitation. It does not settle whether Scorpius's strategic claims are fully trustworthy.[1][2][4]

Snurcher's guide records additional technical and continuity details, including the Command Carrier's attempt to stabilize wormholes with the ship's hull as a wave repeater, the use of immobilizer pulses against Moya, and the carrier's terrain-reconstruction facilities. These are useful secondary leads and should be checked against the episode or production documentation before being treated as authoritative technical specifications.[2]
