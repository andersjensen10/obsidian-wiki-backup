# Into the Lion's Den — Wolf in Sheep's Clothing

## Episode identity

“Into the Lion's Den, Part II: Wolf in Sheep's Clothing” is season 3, episode 21, written by Rockne S. O'Bannon and directed by Rowan Woods. Snurcher's Guide records production number 321, with US and UK air dates of 19 April 2002 and 28 January 2002 respectively.[1] The Farscape Encyclopedia Project gives the corresponding production number as 10321 and identifies the episode as the second part of the Command Carrier story.[4]

The episode is the direct continuation of [[Episodes/Into the Lion's Den - Lambs to the Slaughter]] and precedes “Dog with Two Bones.”[4]

A public YouTube upload matching the episode was recorded as a viewing lead, but no scene timestamps or rights conclusions are asserted from it.[5]

## Canon dossier

### The Command Carrier becomes the target

With Scorpius still pursuing wormhole weapon research and threatening Earth as leverage, John concludes that sabotaging the project from inside the Command Carrier is no longer enough. The remaining option is to destroy the carrier and the research stored aboard it.[2][4]

The plan carries an extraordinary civilian and military risk: the carrier is described as more than a metra long and as holding more than 50,000 people. Rygel supports the plan because no power should possess wormhole weapons; D'Argo objects to the scale of the proposed destruction.[4]

### Crais's double bluff and Talyn's sacrifice

Crais appears to betray John's plan to Scorpius, but this is a double bluff. He uses Scorpius's suspicion to regain access to Talyn and arrange the actual destruction of the carrier.[3][4] The decisive mechanism is Talyn's StarBurst inside the carrier's hangar: the resulting collapse gives the crew time to escape while making Talyn's and Crais's deaths inevitable.[3]

Aeryn is the person Crais trusts to help convince John that the plan is genuine. She also helps free the prisoners, pauses to assist other people evacuating the carrier, and confronts Henta after Henta attempts to kill her.[3]

Crais's final act completes his long-running turn away from Peacekeeper service. The episode presents his death as a deliberate sacrifice, while leaving the precise motive behind his treatment of Larell somewhat open: protection, distrust, or both are possible readings.[3]

### Crichton, Scorpius, and the wormhole knowledge

John takes Scorpius through a wormhole in the Farscape-1 module as a diversion and as a demonstration of the danger of the research. The sequence also lets John access more of the wormhole equations through his subconscious.[1][3] John subsequently uses the Aurora chair to remove the wormhole knowledge from Co-Kura Strappa's mind before escaping the carrier.[3]

John and Scorpius exchange the codes needed to remove their I-Yensch bracelets. The scene does not resolve their conflict: Scorpius walks away into the collapsing carrier, while John returns to Moya and writes the equations into a book.[3][4]

### The crew's separation

The carrier operation sharpens the crew's already visible disagreement about whether to remain together. Chiana plans to seek the Nebari resistance and her brother Nerri; Jool, D'Argo, and Rygel also discuss leaving Moya's crew. The episode therefore combines the destruction of the wormhole project with a temporary emotional and logistical dispersal of the group.[3]

## Production and reception notes

The BBC's contemporary episode guide identifies the central premise as John having to trust Crais to destroy Scorpius's wormhole ambitions, and describes the episode as the culmination of the multi-part Command Carrier story.[2] Snurcher's Guide lists Jool, Braca, Henta, Larell, and Co-Kura Strappa among the guest roles.[1] Its review praises the scale of the conclusion but specifically criticizes the rapid persuasion of the Peacekeeper wormhole scientist as the weak point in an otherwise near-flawless adventure.[2]

Reactor's later rewatch separates recap from interpretation and treats the episode as a major culmination for Crais, Aeryn, Crichton, and Scorpius. Its interpretation is useful for reception history, but its judgments about character motivation and technical implications should not be treated as additional canon.[3]

## Canon / interpretation boundary

- **Canon-level episode claims:** the carrier-destruction plan, Crais and Talyn's sacrifice, John's wormhole flight with Scorpius, the Aurora-chair intervention, and the crew's prospective separation.[3][4]
- **Production/reference claims:** episode numbering, writer, director, production number, cast, and air dates.[1][4]
- **Interpretation:** Crais's redemption arc, the ethical status of destroying a populated carrier, the implied significance of John's survival in the module, and the episode's place among television science-fiction finales.[2][3]
- **Not established here:** an authorized scene-by-scene timestamp audit, an official transcript, the complete technical explanation of Talyn's StarBurst collapse, and a definitive account of Scorpius's escape or later actions.[unverified]
