# Fractures

## Episode identity

“Fractures” is season 3, episode 18 of *Farscape*. The episode was written by Rockne S. O’Bannon and directed by Tony Tilse; the archived transcript records a U.S. airdate of 24 August 2001 and a UK airdate of 14 January 2002.[1][2] The episode’s production number is listed as 10318 in the fan-maintained reference, which is treated here as a cross-check rather than a primary credit record.[3]

## Canon summary

Moya’s crew expects a transport pod from Talyn, but the pod instead carries escaped prisoners: Naj Gil, a Scarran; Hubero, a Nebari; Orrhn, a female Hynerian; Markir Tal, a captured Peacekeeper technician; and the disassembled remains of a Boolite. The transcript’s episode summary identifies the prisoners’ escape as connected to a Peacekeeper weapons test and describes the Boolite’s unusual ability to survive in separated pieces.[1]

Talyn’s crew reunites with Moya later.[1] The reunion is shadowed by the death of the other John Crichton and by Aeryn’s emotional distance from the surviving Crichton.[1] The BBC’s contemporary episode guide treats this reunion as a central parallel storyline rather than mere background to the prisoner mystery.[2]

A Peacekeeper distress signal is transmitted from Moya, Naj Gil is shot, and suspicion moves among the guests. Crais and Jool partially reassemble the Boolite, whose communication confirms that a traitor is present. Orrhn is ultimately revealed as the betrayer: she has been sending signals while Rygel sleeps, releases Markir, and helps abduct Rygel as a bargaining object. Hubero and the Boolite die during the escape attempt; Aeryn and Crichton pursue the transport pod, recover Rygel, and kill Markir.[1][3]

The episode closes on a strategic fracture within Moya’s reunited group.[1] After receiving the other Crichton’s message in Stark’s mask, John decides to confront Scorpius and stop his wormhole research.[1] Aeryn and Crais stand with him, while Pilot, Moya, Chiana, Jool, and Rygel object or withhold support; the BBC identifies this decision as a setup for the season’s remaining plot.[2]

## Nebari and outcast representation

Hubero is introduced as an androgynous Nebari and becomes a point of recognition for Chiana, another Nebari exile. The transcript presents Hubero as someone whom Nebari Prime refused to receive after the Peacekeepers found her, leaving her in a non-conformist colony; it also records Hubero’s self-description as neither male nor female.[1] The episode’s treatment establishes Hubero’s exclusion and Chiana’s solidarity as canon events, but it does not by itself establish the full legal, medical, demographic, or historical structure of Nebari society. Those broader claims belong in [[Worldbuilding/Nebari]] and [[Worldbuilding/Nebari Resistance]] only when supported by additional episode or production evidence.

## Chiana’s precognition

Chiana experiences flashes connected to the shootings of Naj Gil and Hubero, but cannot identify the shooter from the visions. This makes her knowledge dramatically useful but evidentially incomplete: the episode does not turn her precognition into a reliable identification mechanism.[1] Her attempt to hide Hubero also links the mystery plot to Chiana’s own status as a Nebari outsider.[2]

## Production and interpretation boundaries

The archived BBC page is a contemporary broadcaster guide and review, so its plot and credit statements are useful secondary documentation; its comments about pace, mystery construction, and the “superfluous” quality of Hubero’s medical history are criticism, not canon.[2] The Farscape Encyclopedia Project is useful for episode-level discovery, cast leads, and continuity cross-checking, but remains fan-maintained.[3] The Reactor rewatch supplies a later critical synopsis and character interpretation, including the episode’s parallel between the reunited Johns and the fractured crew; those interpretive passages are kept separate from the canon summary.[4]

## Evidence limits and open questions

- No authorized scene-level video audit was completed in this run; the transcript is an archival fan transcript, not an official studio script.[1]
- The episode establishes Hubero’s identity and exclusion, but does not justify a complete account of Nebari policy toward androgens without corroborating episodes or production sources.[1]
- The Boolite’s biology and the Peacekeeper weapon are episode premises, not a complete technical specification.[1][3]
