# Chiana makeup and costume

## Scope

A bounded production slice on how Chiana's on-screen look was developed and maintained: the rejected early makeup ideas, the practical constraints of the final design, the costume's movement requirements, and the currently traceable production credits. This note does not treat fan recollections or unattributed concept-art captions as definitive production records.

## Canon-facing result

Chiana is presented in the series as a Nebari with a pale grey/white complexion, silver hair, dark enlarged irises, and a distinctive physicality. The makeup and costume are production devices rather than separate in-universe technologies; the episodes themselves do not explain their fabrication or assign named designers.

## Production fact: the design changed around Gigi Edgley

The early concept direction reportedly used white and orange tones and was judged less successful when translated into live-action straight makeup. Dave Elsey described the creature-design team as arriving while the actors were still being cast and makeup tests were still in progress.[1]

The Companion's account attributes the final facial approach to makeup-and-hair supervisor Lesley Vanderwalt, who used Edgley's audition tape and facial structure to revise the proposed design. Elsey described the intended effect as a broken-up skin tone rather than a flat or clown-like application.[1] The available evidence supports this as interview testimony and production reporting, not as a surviving studio design document.

The initial concept art is reproduced by The Companion with a Jim Henson Company attribution and a 2002 date, but the artist is not identified in the page text.[1] It is therefore a provenance lead, not a fully resolved credit record.

## Production fact: rejected teeth and contact lenses

Chiana was initially tested with sharp, rodent-like prosthetic teeth. The teeth were abandoned because they interfered with Edgley's speech and caused dribbling; the surviving makeup-test upload is useful visual evidence of the rejected direction, but its uploader and underlying rights status are not independently established.[1][5][6]

The enlarged dark irises were achieved with special contact lenses. Edgley said the lenses restricted her peripheral vision, which contributed to Chiana's need to turn her head farther and helped shape the character's recognizable head movements.[1][2]

The daily transformation was substantial. Edgley described arriving around 4 a.m. and spending roughly three and a half hours in makeup; a contemporary interview also records multiple makeup and optometry fittings during the audition process.[2] The different durations should not be collapsed: the longer figure describes an audition/makeup-trailer experience, while the three-and-a-half-hour figure describes the routine process.

## Production fact: costume and movement

Edgley described Chiana's first costume as having small fluffy shoulders and allowing useful freedom of movement. Later versions needed adjustment because her crouched “Chiana position” could expose the back of the costume.[1]

A Propstore auction record identifies a screen-used Chiana costume as a custom-made grey and beige mesh nylon-blend bodysuit, studded grey leather top, black leather bracers and gloves, matching studded trousers with built-in jodhpurs, black leather greaves, shoes, and a holster. The record is evidence for the construction and labeling of one preserved costume lot, not proof that every Chiana outfit used the same materials.[3]

A separate Propstore catalogue record describes another costume grouping as grey wool and mohair for the top and trousers, with leather gauntlets, greaves, and holster added to complete the display. It also notes a “Stunt” label and a Jim Henson Company certificate of authenticity.[7] The difference between the lots supports the cautious conclusion that Chiana's wardrobe existed in multiple screen-used or production-associated versions, including stunt or replacement components.

## Credit ledger — current confidence

- **Dave Elsey** — credited in the available full-credit listing as a Jim Henson Creature Shop creative supervisor and creature designer; The Companion separately identifies him as creature designer and creative consultant in its interview/report.[1][4]
- **Lesley Vanderwalt** — identified by The Companion as makeup and hair supervisor involved in redesigning Chiana's look around Edgley's face.[1]
- **Terry Ryan** — listed by IMDb as the series costume designer.[4]
- **Jennifer Lamphee** — listed by IMDb as hair designer/makeup designer/makeup supervisor for a 2001–2002 span; this is a series-level credit listing and does not by itself establish which Chiana-specific days or assets she supervised.[4]
- **Additional makeup, prosthetic, hair, costume, and creature-shop personnel** — the full-credit listing contains many department credits, but episode-level screen-credit reconciliation is still incomplete.[4]

## Interpretation

Chiana's design combines three layers: a visually legible alien identity, practical restrictions that became part of Edgley's performance, and a costume built to survive an unusually physical character posture. The head turns and crouched stance should therefore not be treated as purely actor invention or purely makeup effects; the available testimony points to an interaction between lenses, makeup, costume fit, and performance choices.[1][2]

## Fandom and media notes

The original makeup-test video is valuable as a visual lead for the rejected teeth design, while the official Jim Henson Company “Chiana Arrives!” upload is a safer provenance lead for the broadcast character's introduction. Neither video should be treated as a license for downloading or reusing copyrighted footage.[5][6]

## Open questions

- A primary production document naming every Chiana makeup, hair, creature, and costume contributor has not been located.
- The artist behind the reproduced early concept art remains unidentified in the consulted source.
- The exact episode allocation of the Propstore costume lots needs screen-match research.
- A timestamped audit of the official video and the makeup-test video remains pending.
- The full credit listing should be reconciled against episode end credits or an authorized production archive before being treated as definitive.
