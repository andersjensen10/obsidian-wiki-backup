# Nebari Resistance

The Nebari resistance is an anti-Establishment movement associated with [[Characters/Chiana]]’s brother Nerri. Its existence and Nerri’s involvement are established in “A Clockwork Nebari”; its organization, membership, territorial control, and long-term strategy are not.[1][2]

## Episode-grounded canon

Nerri is reported to have led an assault on Nebari freight convoys shortly before the events of “A Clockwork Nebari.”[1][3]
The report identifies him as an active opponent of the Establishment, but does not establish the size of the force involved or whether the assault was a military operation, a raid, or another form of sabotage.[1][2]

Nerri and Chiana were permitted to leave [[Nebari Prime]] under official exit visas. The Establishment had infected them during a routine physical examination as part of its delayed-action contagion program, intending that selected emigrants would spread the infection beyond Nebari space.[1][2]

Nerri later obtained an antibody from someone high in the Establishment. Because he could identify that source, he remained in hiding to protect both the source and the resistance.[1][2]
He and Chiana separated because they would be less conspicuous apart, and because the Establishment could use Chiana to locate him.[1][2]

A message intended for Chiana was carried through resistance contacts rather than delivered directly by Nerri. The message chips could be activated by contact with Chiana’s DNA, and the couriers were instructed not to bring her to Nerri.[1]
This indicates a compartmentalized contact procedure, but the episode does not explain whether that procedure reflects the resistance’s normal structure or only an emergency safeguard.[unverified]

## Relationship to the Establishment

The resistance opposes a government that uses mind cleansing, coercive control technology, and a contagion-based expansion plan.[1][2]
The Establishment’s stated “greater good” justification is presented by the episode as the rationale for coercion rather than as neutral public-health policy.[2][5]

The antibody source inside the Establishment is not named.
No reviewed source establishes whether this person is a formal defector, a covert sympathizer, or an official pursuing an internal factional policy.[1][2][unverified]

The episode also leaves the resistance’s objective beyond opposition unclear.
It does not explicitly state whether Nerri seeks to dismantle the Establishment, expose the contagion plan, distribute the antibody, liberate Nebari citizens, or pursue several of these aims at once.[1][2][5]

## Later appearance: “Fractures”

“Fractures” introduces Hubero, a Nebari escapee who identifies as an androgen and says Nebari Prime rejected her as a non-conformist.[3][4]
Chiana treats Hubero’s marginalization as evidence that she is an outcast under the Establishment, but the episode does not identify Hubero as a member of Nerri’s resistance.[3][4]

Hubero’s presence broadens the evidence for Nebari dissent and persecution beyond Chiana and Nerri.
It does not, by itself, prove that the resistance has a wider organized network among escaped prisoners or nonconformist colonies.[3][4][unverified]

## Evidence boundary

**Established or strongly supported:**

- A Nebari resistance exists.[1][2]
- Nerri is active in it and is associated with an assault on Nebari freight convoys.[1][2]
- An unidentified person high in the Establishment supplied Nerri with an antibody.[1][2]
- Resistance contacts used a DNA-activated message procedure to reach Chiana without exposing Nerri’s location.[1]
- Hubero’s later story confirms another form of Nebari exclusion and escape, but not membership in Nerri’s movement.[3][4]

**Not established by the reviewed sources:**

- The resistance’s name, command structure, membership, headquarters, population support, or territorial control.[unverified]
- Whether the resistance possessed a general cure or only the antibody used for Chiana and Nerri.[unverified]
- Whether Nerri’s convoy assault was part of a sustained campaign or an isolated operation.[unverified]
- Whether the resistance appears again after “Fractures” in the television canon reviewed here.[unverified]

Fan-maintained summaries sometimes describe the movement as a broad revolutionary front and infer its scale or resilience. Those descriptions are useful discovery leads, but this note does not promote them to episode-established fact without additional primary evidence.[2]
