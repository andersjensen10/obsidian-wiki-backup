# Nebari

The Nebari are a humanoid species originating on [[Nebari Prime]].[4]

The episode material establishes them through their government, the Establishment, and through the experiences of [[Characters/Chiana]] and her brother Nerri rather than through a comprehensive physiology profile.[3]

The available reference material describes grey skin, blue blood, and a sex-linked hair-color convention, but those details should remain secondary to the episode-grounded political material.[3][4]

## Episode-grounded canon: A Clockwork Nebari

“ A Clockwork Nebari ” is season 2, episode 18, written by Lily Taylor and directed by Rowan Woods; its US airdate was 15 September 2000.[1][2][3]

The episode begins when Chiana’s past catches up with her and a Nebari group attempts to take her back, using a temporary mind-cleansing drug on members of Moya’s crew.[2][3]

The Establishment presents mind-cleansing as social control.

The long process is described as taking many cycles, while the temporary process uses a drug-delivery system attached behind the eyes or to the optic nerve; the latter does not work on Nebari or Pilot, and it clears more quickly in a Hynerian such as Rygel.[2][5]

The episode also makes John Crichton’s resistance a consequence of Scorpius’s neural implant accelerating the drug’s metabolism.[3][5]

The episode’s larger revelation is that the Establishment has been spreading a dormant contagion through selected Nebari dissidents and other populations.

Chiana and Nerri were permitted to leave Nebari Prime, infected during a routine physical, and only later learned what had happened.

Nerri received an antibody from a person high in the Establishment and became associated with a resistance movement.[2][3]

The episode presents the contagion as a strategic instrument: it is intended to create social chaos before Nebari emissaries arrive, reducing resistance to expansion.[2][3][6]

The Nebari therefore function in this episode as both an authoritarian internal regime and an expansionist external threat. Varla frames forced cleansing as a choice between treatment and death, while the episode’s events aboard Moya show that the Establishment’s claimed “greater good” is inseparable from coercion, bodily violation, and the suppression of dissent.[3][6]

## Physiology and technology

The available canon-oriented reference material attributes blue blood, grey skin, and characteristic white hair for women and black hair for men to the Nebari; it also records that Nebari are unaffected by the temporary chemical mind-cleansing method.[2][4][5] These details are useful reference points, but the vault does not yet have a primary production source or a scene-level audit for each claim.[unverified]

The episode establishes advanced restraint and medical technology: control collars can administer a lethal dose to Pilot if Moya attempts to starburst, and the temporary cleansing system can directly alter the behavior of non-Nebari subjects.[2][3]

The available episode guide also records that Nebari forces destroyed the Peacekeeper vessel Zelbinion, though the precise military context and the relationship between the Nebari and Peacekeepers remain unresolved in this dossier.[2][3]

## Resistance and uncertainty

Nerri’s resistance is episode canon, but the series leaves its organization, scale, and long-term strategy underdeveloped after this episode.[2][3] The contagion’s later status, the identity of the Establishment figure who supplied the antibody, and the extent of Nebari expansion are unresolved here rather than silently filled in from fan summaries.[unverified]

The A.V. Club essay is useful for interpretation: it reads the episode as a shift from morally arguable authoritarian “stabilization” toward a more clearly imperial and genocidal threat.[6]

That is critical analysis, not an in-universe fact.[unverified]

The fan-maintained Nebari and episode pages are discovery and continuity aids; their broader claims should be checked against episodes or production sources before being promoted to settled canon.[3][4][5]
