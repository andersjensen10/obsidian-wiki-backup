# Pilot (species)

## Overview

The Pilots are a humanoid species whose sole biological purpose is to bond with Leviathan-class biomechanoid ships as their translators and nervous-system interface. The species appears bred for this function — their large heads host brains capable of processing independent thought while simultaneously maintaining a deep neural connection to their host Leviathan.[1] Pilots cannot travel from their home planet without being bonded to a Leviathan, and those bred for Peacekeeper service are kept in servitude.[1]

## Biology

### Physical description

- **Head:** Large relative to body size, accommodating an oversized brain.
- **Arms:** Four upper limbs that operate the Leviathan's control console; the arms carry "remarkable strength."[1]
- **Lower body:** Below the console, Pilots have four thin, jointed limbs and nerve tendrils that connect to their host Leviathan's neural interface.
- **Regeneration:** Pilots can regenerate from severe injury quickly — Moya's Pilot has lost his arm and regenerated it; a Pilot aboard the Leviathan Rovhu had his arms eaten off multiple times with rapid regeneration.[1]
- **Lifespan:** Average of 1,000 cycles independently; when bonded to a Leviathan, they share the Leviathan's life cycle of approximately 300 cycles.[1]

### Bonding

The natural bond between a Pilot and Leviathan takes approximately one cycle (roughly one Earth year) to complete. During this period, the Pilot's control and influence over the Leviathan are minimal.[2] The bond is neurally deep: the Pilot feels what the Leviathan feels, interprets her emotions and needs, and serves as her voice to the crew.

Artificial bonding (as the Peacekeepers practiced) causes permanent pain for both participants.[3] Removal of a bonded Pilot is a violent process — when the Peacekeepers forcibly removed Moya's original Pilot, it is implied he was killed.[3]

If the Pilot and Leviathan are not rejoined within roughly one arn after separation, their wounds scab over and the two can never be bonded again, resulting in the death of the unjoined Pilot.[1]

## Society and culture

On their home world, elders of the Pilot society judge which Pilots are destined to bond with Leviathans.[1] There are two paths: voluntary bonding with independent Leviathans (for Pilots seeking freedom and the stars), and Peacekeeper service (for those bred for captivity and servitude).

It is possible that the Pilot elders have a pact with the Peacekeepers to train Pilots for Peacekeeper servitude, or with the Relgarians for transplant to suitable Leviathans.[1] Pilots are in high demand as a species — their parts (brain tissue, nerve tendrils) are valuable for research, and individual Pilots can be bought or sold.

### Voluntary vs. enforced bonding

Most Pilots bond willingly with Leviathans to see the stars and experience freedom. However, Peacekeeper-captured Pilots are bonded under duress to Leviathans they did not choose, often experiencing permanent suffering from the forced connection.[3] The episode "The Way We Weren't" (2.17) shows a flashback of the execution of Moya's original Pilot, establishing the Peacekeepers' brutal enforcement of their control.[3]

## Role on Leviathans

A bonded Pilot serves as:

1. **Translator:** Leviathans communicate primarily through non-verbal means. The Pilot interprets the Leviathan's thoughts, emotions, and needs for the crew, and conveys the crew's intentions to the ship.
2. **Systems controller:** The Pilot maintains life support, minor internal repairs, and other details essential for crew survival but not directly related to the Leviathan's well-being.[6]
3. **Emotional conduit:** The Pilot shares the Leviathan's emotional state and can sense danger, distress, or comfort before the crew can perceive it.

Pilots regularly bond willingly with Leviathans for freedom, though those bred for Peacekeeper service are kept in chains and forced to serve.[1]

## Key canonical appearances

- **Season 1** — The Pilot is a regular character aboard Moya from episode 1.01 "Premiere," interacting with the crew through the DRDs. His relationship with Moya deepens throughout the season.
- **Season 2** — Appears in "The Way We Weren't" (2.17), which shows the flashback of Moya's original Pilot's execution by the Peacekeepers. The Season 2 finale "I,ETCetera" (2.18) reveals the crew-Pilot shared consciousness.
- **Season 3** — Continues as a regular aboard Moya through the "Into the Lion's Den" arc.
- **Season 4** — Remains aboard Moya throughout the final season.
