# Mind Cleansing

## Scope

In *Farscape*, “mind cleansing” is the Nebari Establishment’s coercive method of altering or suppressing a subject’s behavior and loyalties.[5] The surviving evidence supports two forms shown in the series: a long-form process associated with Durka, and a temporary drug-induced procedure used in “A Clockwork Nebari.”[3] The note distinguishes episode-grounded canon from interpretation and from fan-maintained reference material.

## Canon shown in the episodes

### Long-form cleansing: “Durka Returns”

The episode identifies Durka as having undergone Nebari mental cleansing before he arrives on Moya.[2][4] Snurcher’s episode guide calls the process “neural realignment,” describes it as taking more than 100 cycles under cryonic suspension, and glosses mental cleansing as Nebari sociopolitical reeducation.[1] The same guide’s episode summary says that Durka reverts to his former personality after Salis is murdered, establishing that the procedure’s apparent reform is not irreversible.[2]

The episode’s central conflict treats the process as an assault on identity rather than ordinary rehabilitation: Chiana is threatened with the same fate and would rather die than undergo it.[2] That framing is consistent with the episode’s political premise, but the available guide material does not establish a technical mechanism beyond cryonic suspension and neural realignment.[1][2]

### Temporary drug-induced cleansing: “A Clockwork Nebari”

“A Clockwork Nebari” presents a distinct, temporary version. Chiana explains that Varla’s procedure is drug-induced and that its effects wear off as the body metabolizes the drug.[3][5] The episode’s plot summary records that Aeryn, D’Argo, and Zhaan eventually recover, while Rygel’s unusually fast metabolism allows him to recover earlier; Crichton resists the procedure because of Harvey’s neural interference.[5]

The temporary process is used on non-Nebari members of Moya’s crew as part of an attempted forced return of Chiana.[5] The Nebari also restrain Pilot with a collar that can kill him if Moya starbursts, making the cleansing operation part of a larger coercive seizure rather than an isolated medical intervention.[5]

## What the process does — and does not establish

- **Behavioral conformity:** The Establishment presents cleansing as a way to eliminate violent or aggressive behavior and make subjects conform to approved norms.[5]
- **Memory and identity:** The episodes show altered behavior, emotional presentation, and apparent loyalty, but they do not give a complete account of what memories remain accessible after either form.[2][5]
- **Reversibility:** The long-form conditioning can be broken in Durka’s case; the temporary form expires through metabolism. These are different failure modes and should not be treated as the same process.[2][5]
- **Biological specificity:** “A Clockwork Nebari” depicts the temporary drug working differently across species and individuals, but the source record does not establish a general pharmacological rule for all non-Nebari species.[5]
- **Political function:** The procedure is not neutral public-health care in the episodes. It is administered without meaningful consent to enforce conformity and remove resistance. This is an interpretation of the depicted actions, supported by the Establishment’s stated objective and the coercive circumstances.[5]

## Terminology and evidence boundaries

“Mind cleansing,” “mental cleansing,” and “neural realignment” are related terms in the available episode-guide evidence, but they should not be assumed to be three independently documented technologies. The strongest bounded description is: long-form Nebari neural realignment under cryonic suspension in “Durka Returns,” contrasted with a temporary drug-induced cleansing in “A Clockwork Nebari.”[1][5]

The Farscape Encyclopedia Project is useful for locating scene and continuity details, including the sequence of recoveries and the Nerri/contagion plot, but it is a fan-maintained reference and is therefore treated here as a discovery and cross-check source rather than sole authority.[5] The critical reading of “A Clockwork Nebari” is interpretive rather than an in-universe technical source; it is useful for discussing authoritarian conformity, not for adding mechanics to the procedure.[5]

## Open questions

- The exact on-screen wording and scene timestamps for both procedures still need a primary-footage audit.[unverified]
- No primary production document has been located here that defines the fictional science of neural realignment.[unverified]
- The relationship between the Nebari contagion program and cleansing remains political/plot context, not evidence that cleansing itself transmits or removes the contagion.[5]
